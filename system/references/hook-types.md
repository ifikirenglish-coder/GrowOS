# Hook Formulas

Opening sequences that capture attention and pull readers into your content. Used by `/landing-page`, `/newsletter-write`, `/article-write`, and other content skills.

---

## The 5 Hook Types

### Type 1: Problem Agitation
**Focus:** Dive into their pain and make them feel understood.
**Structure:**
1. State the frustrating situation they're in
2. Pile on the specifics of the pain
3. Acknowledge how it makes them feel
4. Hint that it doesn't have to be this way

**Example:**
> You're staring at your analytics dashboard again.
>
> The numbers don't make sense. Traffic is up, sales are down, and you have no idea what to change. So you do what you always do. Tweak something random and hope it works.
>
> Meanwhile, you've got a business to run. Clients to serve. A life outside this screen.
>
> Here's the thing: It's not your fault. And there IS another way.

**Best for:** Acute pain, need to feel understood before they'll listen

### Type 2: Story Opening
**Focus:** Draw them in with a specific, relatable narrative.
**Structure:**
1. Open with a specific moment or scene
2. Build tension or curiosity
3. Connect the story to their situation
4. Transition toward the insight/solution

**Example:**
> Last Tuesday, I got a message from a customer that stopped me cold.
>
> "I spent $2,000 on marketing this month. I have no idea if it worked."
>
> $2,000. Gone. And the only thing they knew for sure was that they were more confused than when they started.

**Best for:** When you have a relevant story, building credibility through shared experience

### Type 3: Bold Statement
**Focus:** Challenge assumptions or make a provocative claim.
**Structure:**
1. Make a surprising or counterintuitive statement
2. Back it up with brief rationale
3. Challenge what they've been told
4. Set up the "new truth" you'll reveal

**Example:**
> Most marketing advice is keeping you broke.
>
> Not because the gurus are lying. But because they're teaching you to be a media buyer when you should be running a business.
>
> You don't need more tools. You need fewer, better ones.

**Best for:** Saturated markets, frustrated audiences, genuinely different perspective

### Type 4: Question Sequence
**Focus:** Pull readers in with questions that demand mental engagement.
**Structure:**
1. Open with a question they're definitely asking
2. Follow with related questions (builds momentum)
3. Acknowledge the frustration of not having answers
4. Hint that answers exist

**Example:**
> What if you could know, for certain, whether your marketing was working?
>
> What if, instead of guessing, you could get a simple answer: "Keep this. Kill that. Change this."
>
> What if your weekly review took 5 minutes instead of 5 hours?
>
> The answers are simpler than you think.

**Best for:** Questions matching their internal dialogue, active solution-seekers

### Type 5: Future Vision
**Focus:** Paint a vivid picture of life after the transformation.
**Structure:**
1. "Imagine..." or "Picture this..." opening
2. Describe specific scenes from their desired future
3. Make it feel real and achievable
4. Connect back to their current reality
5. Bridge to "here's how"

**Example:**
> Picture this: It's Monday morning. You grab your coffee, open your laptop, and check your marketing.
>
> But instead of confusion, you see clarity. Three simple insights. Three simple actions. Done in under 5 minutes.
>
> The rest of your day? Actually running your business.

**Best for:** Need hope more than education, highly desirable transformation

## Transition Phrases

Every hook needs to flow naturally into the next section.

**Problem → Solution:**
- "Here's the thing..."
- "But there's another way..."
- "That's when I discovered..."

**Story → Credibility:**
- "After [experience], I learned..."
- "Here's what changed everything..."

**Bold Statement → Evidence:**
- "Don't believe me? Let me show you..."
- "Here's what I mean..."

**Questions → Answers:**
- "The answers are simpler than you think."
- "It all comes down to..."

**Future Vision → Path:**
- "And it's closer than you think."
- "Here's how to get there."

## Scoring Rubric

Score hooks on 5 criteria (1-10 each):

1. **Attention Grab** — Does it capture attention in the first line?
2. **Emotional Resonance** — Does it create a genuine emotional response?
3. **Smooth Transition** — Does it flow naturally into the rest of the content?
4. **Voice Authenticity** — Does it match the brand's voice profile?
5. **Audience Match** — Does it speak directly to the target audience?

Generate one of each type, score all 5, select top 2 for testing.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Throat-clearing:** "In today's competitive marketplace..." → Just start.
2. **Lecture tone:** "You need to understand that..." → "Let me guess..."
3. **Feature dump:** "Our platform offers..." → "What if you knew exactly..."
4. **Generic pain:** "Marketing is hard." → "Last month you spent $2,000. Can you name one thing that worked?"
5. **No transition:** Hook ends abruptly → Always bridge to next section.
