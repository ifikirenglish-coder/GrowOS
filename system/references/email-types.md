# Email Types

Templates for different email formats. Used by `/newsletter-write` and `/email-sequence`.

---

## Single Emails

### Value/Teaching Email
For: Sharing a lesson, framework, principle, or useful resource.
```
1. Hook — Question or intriguing statement (1-3 lines)
2. Personal context — Why this is on your mind (2-3 short paragraphs)
3. Core lesson — The main insight with examples (the meat)
4. Practical application — "Here's how you can use this" with specific steps
5. Engagement CTA — "Hit reply and let me know..." or a question
6. Sign-off
7. P.S. — Secondary resource, reply prompt, or tease
```

### Story-to-Lesson Email
For: Sharing a personal experience that leads to a business insight.
```
1. Story opener — Drop into the action or set the scene (1-3 lines)
2. Build the narrative — Specific details, show vulnerability
3. Turning point — The "aha" moment or unexpected outcome
4. The lesson — What this teaches (keep it practical)
5. Reader application — "Here's my question to you:" or "What can you simplify?"
6. Engagement CTA — Invite reply
7. Sign-off
8. P.S. — Best-read part. Use for secondary offer, resource, or follow-up tease
```

### Observation Email
For: A quick take on something you noticed.
```
1. Observation — State what you noticed (1-2 paragraphs)
2. Why it matters — Connect to reader's world (2-3 paragraphs)
3. One takeaway — A single actionable thought
4. Quick CTA — Reply or resource
5. Sign-off
```

### Case Study / Proof Email
For: Showing a result, transformation, or customer win.
```
1. Hook — The result or "before" state (attention-grabbing)
2. Context — What was happening before (the struggle)
3. What changed — The approach or decision that made the difference
4. The result — Specific outcomes (numbers if possible)
5. Lesson for reader — What they can take from this
6. CTA — If promoting: natural transition to offer
7. Sign-off
```

### Curiosity / Cliffhanger Email
For: Building anticipation for something coming.
```
1. Open loop — Tease something interesting without revealing it
2. Context — Why this matters to them
3. Partial reveal — Give enough to satisfy but leave them wanting more
4. Promise — What's coming next
5. CTA — "Keep an eye out" or "Reply if you want early access"
6. Sign-off
```

## Email Sequence Types

### Welcome Sequence (5-7 emails)
**Goal:** Turn new subscriber into engaged reader + introduce your world.

| Email | Timing | Purpose |
|-------|--------|---------|
| 1 | Immediately | Deliver lead magnet + introduce yourself. Personal, warm. |
| 2 | Day 1 | Your story. Why you do what you do. Build connection. |
| 3 | Day 3 | Best lesson/framework. Prove you're worth reading. |
| 4 | Day 5 | Common mistake they're making. Show understanding. |
| 5 | Day 7 | Results/proof. Someone who followed your advice. |
| 6 | Day 10 | Soft offer. "If you want help with this..." |
| 7 | Day 14 | Check-in. "How's it going?" Engagement focus. |

### Nurture Sequence (ongoing)
**Goal:** Stay top-of-mind and build trust between launches.

Rotate through email types: Value → Story → Observation → Value → Proof → Value → Promote (1:6 ratio)

### Launch Sequence (7-10 emails over 7-14 days)
**Goal:** Convert engaged audience into buyers during a launch window.

| Phase | Emails | Purpose |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Pre-launch (3) | Seed the problem, share your journey, tease the solution |
| Cart open (3-4) | Announce, explain the offer, handle objections, share proof |
| Cart close (3) | Urgency (real deadline), final objection handling, last call |

### Re-engagement Sequence (3-4 emails)
**Goal:** Win back subscribers who stopped opening.

| Email | Approach |
|-------|----------|
| 1 | "Been a while. Here's what you missed" (curiosity) |
| 2 | Best piece of content from last 30 days (value) |
| 3 | Direct ask: "Still interested in [topic]? Reply yes or I'll clean the list" |
| 4 | Final: "Removing you in 48h unless you want to stay" |

## Email Writing Rules

1. **Subject lines:** Short (4-8 words), curiosity-driven, no spam triggers. Lowercase preferred.
2. **Opening line:** No "Hey [Name]!" or "Hope you're doing well." Start with something interesting.
3. **Paragraphs:** 1-3 sentences max. White space is your friend.
4. **Links:** Maximum 1-2 per email. Don't overwhelm with choices.
5. **CTA:** One primary call-to-action. Everything else is secondary.
6. **P.S.:** Use it. It's the second most-read part of any email after the subject line.
7. **Length:** 300-600 words for regular emails. Launch emails can go longer.
8. **Sign-off:** Keep it personal. First name only. No "Best regards" or "Sincerely."
