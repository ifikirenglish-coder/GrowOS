# Headline Formulas

30+ proven headline types for landing pages, ads, emails, and social posts. Used by `/landing-page`, `/ads-create-fb`, `/newsletter-write`, and other content skills.

---

## The 9 Core Types

### Type 1: Outcome-Focused
**Formula:** How to [achieve outcome] in [timeframe] [without common pain]
**Examples:**
- "How to Get 10 Qualified Leads Per Week Without Touching Your Ads"
- "Get More Clients in 30 Days Than You Did All Last Quarter"
**Best for:** Audience knows the problem and wants the solution

### Type 2: Mechanism-Focused
**Formula:** The [Named Mechanism] That [Achieves Outcome] [Unique Angle]
**Examples:**
- "The 3-Question Framework That Replaces 10 Hours of Analysis"
- "Discover the 'Profit Pattern' That Turned My Failed Ads Profitable"
**Best for:** Sophisticated markets that have heard claims before

### Type 3: Problem-Focused
**Formula:** Stop [pain/struggle] and Start [desired state]
**Examples:**
- "Stop Guessing What's Wrong With Your Ads. Start Knowing."
- "Tired of Staring at Dashboards and Feeling Confused?"
**Best for:** Acute pain, problem-aware but not solution-aware audiences

### Type 4: Curiosity-Focused
**Formula:** The [Counterintuitive Truth/Hidden Factor] Behind [Desired Result]
**Examples:**
- "Why Your Best Campaigns Are Actually Costing You Money"
- "The Hidden Metric That Predicts Success (Most Experts Miss It)"
**Best for:** Genuine insights, audiences who have tried other solutions

### Type 5: Proof-Focused
**Formula:** [Specific Proof Point] Reveals [What They Can Achieve]
**Examples:**
- "How I Turned $100 into $1,000 Using One Simple Question"
- "What 847 Successful Campaigns Have in Common"
**Best for:** Skeptical audiences, strong specific proof available

### Type 6: Minimalist Statement
**Formula:** [3-5 words. Period.]
**Examples:**
- "Clarity in 5 minutes."
- "Marketing that makes sense."
**Best for:** Sophisticated audiences who distrust hype

### Type 7: Reframe
**Formula:** It's not [what they think]. It's [new frame].
**Examples:**
- "You don't need more data. You need the right 3 metrics."
- "The problem isn't your budget. It's your clarity."
**Best for:** Audiences stuck in the wrong paradigm

### Type 8: Contrast
**Formula:** [Common approach] vs. [Your approach]
**Examples:**
- "47 metrics vs. 3 questions."
- "Hours of confusion vs. 5 minutes of clarity."
**Best for:** When your approach is dramatically simpler

### Type 9: Desire/Relief
**Formula:** What If [Their Pain/Burden] Was Just... [Resolved/Gone/Handled]?
**Examples:**
- "What If Your Marketing Was Just... Handled?"
- "What If Finding Clients Was Just... Automatic?"
**Best for:** Exhausted audiences, done-for-you or system-replacement offers

## Sub-Headline Pairing

Every main headline needs a supporting sub-headline that clarifies, expands, targets, or promises.

| Headline Type | Best Sub-Headline Approach |
|---------------|---------------------------|
| Outcome | Add specificity or mechanism hint |
| Mechanism | State the outcome clearly |
| Problem | Offer hope or solution preview |
| Curiosity | Ground with specific outcome |
| Proof | Make it relatable to reader |
| Minimalist | Expand with specific benefit |
| Reframe | Explain what the new frame means for them |
| Contrast | Specify what they get on the "new way" side |
| Desire/Relief | Explain what "handled" means concretely |

## Pre-Headline Qualifier (Optional)

A short line above the main headline that filters the audience.

**Format:** ALL CAPS, 5-12 words
**Formula:** FOR [SPECIFIC AUDIENCE] WHO [SPECIFIC SITUATION]

**Examples:**
- "FOR BUSINESS OWNERS RUNNING THEIR OWN MARKETING"
- "FOR COACHES WHO ARE TIRED OF CHASING CLIENTS"
- "FOR ECOMMERCE BRANDS SPENDING $1K+/MO ON ADS"

Use when the headline itself doesn't clearly identify who it's for.

## Scoring Rubric

Score headlines on 5 criteria (1-10 each):

1. **Specificity** — How concrete vs. vague? Numbers and specific outcomes score high.
2. **Curiosity** — Does it create an urge to read more? Open loops score high.
3. **Benefit Clarity** — Is the value obvious within 2 seconds?
4. **Audience Match** — Does the target audience feel "this is for ME"?
5. **Differentiation** — Does it stand out from competitor messaging?

**Strong headlines score 7.5+ composite.** Generate all 9 types, score each, select top 5 for testing.

## Awareness Level Matching

| Awareness Level | Best Headline Types |
|-----------------|---------------------|
| 1 - Unaware | Problem, Curiosity |
| 2 - Problem-aware | Problem, Outcome |
| 3 - Solution-aware | Mechanism, Outcome, Reframe |
| 4 - Product-aware | Proof, Mechanism, Contrast |
| 5 - Most aware | Proof, Minimalist, Contrast, Reframe, Desire/Relief |

Most B2B audiences in 2026 are Level 4-5. Default to Level 5 unless evidence suggests otherwise.

## Common Mistakes

1. **Vague promise:** "Transform Your Business" → Better: "Add $10K Monthly Revenue in 90 Days"
2. **Feature headline:** "Advanced AI Analytics" → Better: "Know Exactly Which Ads to Kill"
3. **"We" headline:** "We Help Businesses Grow" → Better: "Grow Your Business Without the Confusion"
4. **Overused format:** "The Ultimate Guide to X" → Better: "The 5-Minute Routine That Fixed My X"
5. **Impossible claim:** "10X Your Revenue Overnight" → Better: "10X Your Clarity in Week One"
